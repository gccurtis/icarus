# Design - Emotional & Aesthetic Doctrine

> Source mirror. Preserve this page as planning evidence; use the agent-facing execution packet when one exists.

<callout icon="✦" color="blue_bg">
	**Reviewed for Taurus Yesod — governing design authority as of July 28, 2026.** This document defines the intended emotional experience. It governs visual-system choices unless superseded by a later explicit founder decision. Taurus Alpha is an implementation mirror, not the source of truth.
</callout>
> **North star:** Taurus should feel like **a quiet workspace with infinite volume**—a weightless futuristic citadel formed from and resting within a sea of clouds, whose clarity and responsive coherence let it become a natural extension of thought.
# The emotional promise
Taurus is a place where consequential work can feel calm, legible, and continuously held. It should very quickly feel like a place the user has been before, even though its capabilities exceed familiar office software.
The product should leave the user feeling:
- **Oriented:** I know where I am, what is active, and where to look next.
- **Unburdened:** the interface carries incidental complexity so I can carry judgment.
- **Capable:** substantial power is nearby without pressing itself into view.
- **Spacious:** there is room to think without losing useful density.
- **Protected:** mistakes are recoverable, derived work is inspectable, and important state is honest.
- **Continuous:** research, analysis, editing, and delivery feel like one unfolding process.
- **Quietly inspired:** the environment has enough celestial character and beauty to invite ambitious work without becoming theatrical.
- **Sustained:** the product is easy to stay with; it does not drain energy through clutter, friction, visual noise, or repeated reorientation.
Calm is not emptiness. Weightlessness is not lack of structure. Intuitiveness is not the absence of learning. Taurus should make its structure easy to internalize, then remain stable enough to become almost subconscious. The highest standard is not merely that the interface is easy to enter, but that it remains easy to inhabit for hours.
# The cognitive-fit model
## The product as a mental habitat
A product exists partly in the user's mind. The interface succeeds when its structure can be compressed into a simple, durable mental model: a few stable places, familiar object behaviors, and predictable transitions. The product should feel less like a machine that must be remembered and more like a place the user can inhabit.
The central design question is:
> **Can the user's attention move through Taurus with the ease of moving through a familiar room?**
This requires a stable mental topology:
- the work remains at the center;
- context enters from the left;
- detail and consequence are inspected on the right;
- AI coordination remains nearby without becoming the room;
- actions occur close to the object they affect;
- transitions preserve the user's place, selection, and causal understanding; and
- the same kind of object behaves the same way across documents, spreadsheets, slides, chat, and future resources.
Once learned, locations and behaviors should not drift merely to make a screen look novel. Familiarity is a cumulative asset.
## Recognition carries the load
Taurus should favor recognition, locality, and visible causality over recall:
- use familiar names and control shapes for familiar actions;
- show the current object and its important state;
- preserve selections and scroll positions through reversible transitions;
- keep essential actions visible or immediately adjacent;
- disclose advanced capability in layers; and
- let keyboard fluency accelerate an understandable interface rather than rescue an obscure one.
The goal is not “zero UI.” The goal is **zero unnecessary negotiation with the UI**.
# The singular aesthetic metaphor
> **Taurus is a futuristic citadel formed from and resting within a sea of clouds.**
This is the only governing visual metaphor.
The citadel is luminous, weightless, refined, and majestic. In some atmospheric conditions it may appear nearly white; in others it may carry pearl, warm mineral, cool metallic-blue, or restrained sunset color reflected from its environment. Whiteness is not the identity. The identity is a coherent formation that feels native to its sky, never heavy or foreign despite its scale.
The metaphor is not an instruction to draw a citadel or clouds. It defines how the complete product should feel.
# Beauty through total form
The citadel's sophistication does not come from dense ornament, complex imagery, or visible machinery. It comes from the elegance of its formation and organization.
Each part carries:
- smoothness;
- refinement;
- clear proportion;
- exact relationship to neighboring parts;
- restrained detail;
- a sense of purpose.
No individual component needs to announce that it is futuristic. When the parts are seen together, their alignment, rhythm, curvature, light, and behavior produce a majestic whole.
This yields a central composition law:
> **The sophistication of Taurus should emerge more strongly from relationships between parts than from decoration applied to any one part.**
A button can remain a clear button. A panel can remain a clear panel. A document can remain a readable document. The futuristic quality appears in how consistently and gracefully the whole system is formed.
# The cloud quality
Cloud does not mean fog, blur, softness without boundaries, literal cloud texture, or a white-only palette. It means:
- luminous and low-glare surfaces;
- weight without heaviness;
- open space that remains connected;
- layers that appear suspended rather than stacked as blocks;
- boundaries expressed through light, tonal change, and position before hard lines;
- forms that curve into one another without losing precision;
- visual atmosphere that supports the work instead of obscuring it.
Celestial expresses this quality through an atmospheric surface field: pearl and warm tan at the base, clearer work surfaces, cool or chromed blue where orientation benefits, and restrained sunset warmth where judgment or ceremony requires it. The result may be pale, but it must not become sterile or uniformly white.
# Two chromatic skies
Taurus has one design system and two chromatic environments:
- **Celestial** is the light environment: the cloud citadel reflecting daylight, atmosphere, and occasional sunset warmth.
- **Night** is the dark environment: the same citadel translated into focused cyberspace, where curves read as flows of data and the surrounding space suggests zero-gravity digital movement.
They are not opposites, and Night is not an inversion of Celestial. Shell topology, component geometry, spacing, typography, disclosure, interaction, resource signatures, and motion remain identical. Only the color layer changes: surfaces, text, boundaries, semantic aliases, shadows, scrims, halos, gradients, and the operating-system `color-scheme` declaration.
Night should support hyper-productive concentration without being shrouded in darkness. It uses a visible blue structural ladder, controlled luminous accents, and slightly sharper—but never abrasive—contrast. *Neuromancer* is permitted only as a reference for inhabitable cyberspace and concentrated technical flow, not for grime, danger, clutter, hacker decoration, or neon spectacle.
Smooth motion retains the same timing and curves in both environments. In Night, color makes that motion read as zero-gravity digital flow; the motion model itself does not change.
# The citadel quality
Citadel means sanctuary, order, permanence, orientation, and the ability to hold substantial complexity without exposing it all at once.
Translate it through:
- a strong, unmistakable central work plane;
- stable regions and dependable landmarks;
- clear thresholds between context, work, inspection, and coordination;
- repeatable geometry;
- visible provenance and recoverability;
- progressive disclosure;
- structural coherence across every resource.
The citadel must never become fortress-like, monumental, bureaucratic, or oppressive. Its strength is expressed as calm organization.
# Futurism through invisible sophistication
The futuristic character comes from high capability organized with such elegance that complexity does not become visual burden.
Translate it through:
- powerful functions appearing exactly when relevant;
- exact grids, geometry, and alignment;
- interfaces that anticipate the information needed for the current object;
- local, inspectable AI;
- transitions that preserve identity and causal understanding;
- a token system that keeps every part perceptually related;
- behavior that feels advanced because it is coherent, not because it is unfamiliar.
Do not translate futurism through command-center density, saturated cyberpunk, fake instrumentation, technical ornament, or small unlabeled controls.
# Immanent responsiveness, transcendent appearance
Taurus can feel biologic without depicting biology.
Its responsive intelligence should feel **immanent**: present within the surface, close to the user's intention, and continuous with the object being touched or edited. A selected object responds locally. A panel unfolds from its source. A transition preserves identity. The system appears to understand where attention already is.
That immanent responsiveness produces a **transcendent appearance**. The product feels elevated or almost angelic because its parts coordinate so completely and respond so naturally—not because it invokes religious imagery, living tissue, creatures, or supernatural spectacle.
This is the intended paradox:
> **Taurus feels transcendent because its intelligence is intimately present, not because it is distant from the user.**
The product should feel touchable, responsive, and continuous with the user while remaining serene and visually pure.
# Formation rules
## 1. The whole precedes the ornament
Establish hierarchy, alignment, rhythm, scale, and flow before adding atmosphere, gradient, texture, or special effects.
## 2. Every part inherits the whole
A component belongs when its contour, spacing, motion, typography, and state behavior reinforce the same refinement as the complete screen.
## 3. Weightlessness requires structure
The interface feels light because relationships are clear and unnecessary competition is absent—not because boundaries, labels, or controls have disappeared.
## 4. Curves draw the user inward
Use curves to guide attention, soften transitions, and connect layers. Retain exact internal geometry so the interface remains professional and legible.
## 5. Response stays close to intention
Feedback, detail, and movement originate near the object or action that caused them. The interface should appear continuous with the user's focus.
## 6. Majesty is reserved for the whole
Individual components remain restrained. Stronger beauty emerges at the level of complete screens, resource transitions, entry, presentation, and finished-work moments.
# Default intensity
Use these as review axes, not CSS values:
```plain text
Cloud luminosity          3 / 5  — luminous and layered, never glaring
Citadel organization      5 / 5  — every part belongs to a stable whole
Futuristic sophistication 4 / 5  — advanced through elegance and capability
Responsive continuity     4 / 5  — local, smooth, and close to intention
Biologic suggestion       1 / 5  — felt only through responsiveness and unity
Visual density            3 / 5  — professional density with clear hierarchy
Motion activity           1 / 5  — still by default; movement explains change
Motion smoothness         5 / 5  — every permitted transition feels continuous
```
# Governing design laws
## 1. The work carries the visual weight
The document, spreadsheet, deck, source, or conversation is the center of gravity. Chrome supports the artifact and recedes. Taurus must never resemble a dashboard surrounding a small patch of actual work.
## 2. Familiar structure carries the cloud citadel
Use conventional affordances for familiar tasks. Put Taurus's originality into total formation, spatial coherence, material behavior, transitions, and resource integration—not into renaming ordinary actions or hiding them behind novel gestures. Beauty should increase fluency, not ask the user to decode the product.
## 3. Spatial memory is sacred
Do not move a known control, panel, or object merely because a different resource has room for it. Preserve stable shell geography and resource-local patterns. When a layout must change, animate the causal transition and preserve the user's focus.
## 4. Spaciousness comes from hierarchy, not deletion
Whitespace, grouping, readable measure, and progressive disclosure should reduce competition. Do not create a sparse composition by making necessary controls distant, hidden, or unlabeled.
## 5. Softness must remain precise
Ambient fields may be warm, low-glare, and gentle. Interaction states must be exact. Focus, selection, editability, sync, failure, provenance, and destructive consequence cannot be vague.
## 6. Curves welcome; grids coordinate
Use nested curves and softened containers for approachability, continuity, and sanctuary. Use straight alignment, tables, baselines, and exact geometry inside them for the instrument. Excessive pills and arbitrary blobs weaken both ideas.
## 7. Color orients; it does not decorate
Semantic color should be sparse enough to retain meaning. Neutral surfaces carry the atmosphere. Action, focus, intelligence, judgment, success, and danger each have stable roles and must not be swapped for novelty.
## 8. Intelligence is ambient, local, and inspectable
AI should appear through useful work, nearby suggestions, connected context, and provenance. It should not demand a chat-first relationship, persistent glow, or a special visual caste for every derived object.
## 9. Disclosure follows intent
Show what is needed for the current decision. Reveal more when the user approaches a relevant object or asks for detail. Essential and safety-critical actions cannot depend only on hover, right-click, or memory.
## 10. Motion preserves continuity
Motion should explain origin, destination, relationship, or status. Prefer unfold, glide, and settle. Avoid bounce, parallax, endless pulse, decorative drift, and movement that competes with reading.
## 11. State is honest and recoverable
The user should understand what changed, whether it is saved, who or what produced it, and how to undo or inspect it. Quietness cannot conceal uncertainty.
## 12. Accessibility is part of the aesthetic
Legible type, visible focus, adequate contrast, non-color state cues, target size, reflow, high contrast, and reduced motion are expressions of calm confidence—not exceptions to the visual system.
# Anti-clutter doctrine
Clutter is not merely “many things.” It is competition without useful organization. A dense spreadsheet can be calm; five equally emphasized cards can be chaotic. Taurus should be **easy to stay with**: the interface should not steadily consume attention or energy simply by remaining on screen.
Every region should have:
1. one clear subject;
2. one primary action or next decision;
3. a visible hierarchy between persistent, contextual, and transient information;
4. no more color or elevation than is required to express that hierarchy; and
5. a clear reason for every persistent boundary.
When reducing clutter, remove competition before removing capability:
- group related controls;
- lower the contrast of stable support;
- collapse infrequent detail;
- keep actions near their objects;
- reuse known visual roles;
- eliminate redundant labels and containers;
- preserve the user's active context.
# Prohibited outcomes
Reject a direction if it becomes:
- generic white-and-blue SaaS;
- saturated cyberpunk or neon command center;
- literal celestial, religious, or franchise-derived ornament;
- sterile minimalism that hides capability;
- glassmorphism used as a default material;
- floating-card fragmentation;
- pill-shaped everything;
- ambient animation that makes the product feel alive at the user's expense;
- AI spectacle;
- “calm” achieved by low contrast or ambiguous state;
- biology, creature-like behavior, menace, horror, surveillance, or predation;
- a different product grammar for every resource.
# Research basis and interpretation
These sources inform the doctrine; they do not mechanically prove a visual style:
- [Processing fluency and aesthetic pleasure](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327957PSPR0804_3) supports legible figure-ground structure, repetition, prototypicality, and coherent patterns as contributors to easier processing and positive aesthetic response.
- [Visual complexity and prototypicality in first impressions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003) found that low visual complexity and high prototypicality produced stronger aesthetic ratings, with effects appearing extremely quickly. For Taurus, novelty should live inside a recognizable interaction structure.
- [Measuring visual clutter](https://doi.org/10.1167/7.2.17) links excess or disorganized display features to harder segmentation, recognition, and visual search. Taurus should manage feature competition, not pursue emptiness.
- [Use a Consistent Visual Design](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o1p03-consistent-design/) explains how consistent position, appearance, state, and structure reduce relearning and errors, particularly for people with cognitive disabilities.
- [The restorative benefits of nature](https://doi.org/10.1016/0272-4944(95)90001-2) describes “extent,” “compatibility,” and softly held attention as parts of restorative environments. Taurus uses these as a conceptual lens for infinite volume, cognitive fit, and non-demanding atmosphere—not as a claim that an interface is equivalent to nature.
- [Humans Prefer Curved Visual Objects](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01759.x) found a general preference for curved over sharp-contoured neutral objects, consistent with using curves to soften approach. Individual and contextual differences remain important; critical boundaries may still be rectilinear.
- [Color Psychology: Effects of Perceiving Color](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115035) cautions against universal, context-free color claims. Palette choices must be validated in the whole interface.
- [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) supplies the binding accessibility floor.
# Validation
Do not ask users whether Taurus “looks celestial.” Test whether the intended experience emerges without prompting.
## Qualitative prompts
After realistic work, ask:
- What felt visually central?
- Where did you hesitate?
- What did you expect to happen next?
- What felt calm, tense, crowded, sterile, or distracting?
- Did the product feel familiar, smooth, generic, or distinctively Taurus?
- Did any effect call attention to itself instead of the work?
- Could you explain where context, detail, and AI assistance live?
## Behavioral checks
Measure:
- time to identify the primary work object and next action;
- errors caused by ambiguous state or hidden actions;
- ability to return to a prior location;
- success after switching resource types;
- use of spatial memory over repeated sessions;
- focus visibility, contrast, target size, zoom/reflow, and reduced-motion behavior;
- sustained-use comfort, perceived effort, and ability to remain focused—not only first-impression preference.
# Design-authority workflow
The design system is document-led:
```plain text
Founder intent or validated evidence
    → update the governing Yesod document
    → record the semantic token or interaction contract
    → update implementation mirrors in Taurus Alpha
    → run visual, behavioral, and accessibility validation
    → feed findings back into Yesod
```
Code can expose implementation constraints and test results, but an existing CSS value does not become design authority merely because it ships.
# Review sentence
Every major visual decision should complete this sentence:
> **This helps Taurus feel like a weightless citadel in the clouds whose organization, beauty, and responsiveness fit naturally inside the user's mind because…**
If the explanation depends mainly on fashion, metaphor, or screenshot appeal, the decision is not ready.
